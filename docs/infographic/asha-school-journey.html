PathShala · a school merger, seen through a child's eyes

Asha's school may close. Which school should she go to?

When a small school merges into another, the nearest school is not always the right one. PathShala listens to parents, reads the ground, checks the rules and the cost, and gives the district officer reasons.

Asha is a fictional child. The schools, the 22 children, the terrain and the citizen messages are PathShala's demo data (GPS Kasta, Kullu district, Himachal Pradesh); the rules and the news facts are real and cited at the bottom.

1 Before

A small school, close to home

Asha is 8. She goes to GPS Kasta, a hill school with 22 children, 2 teachers and 3 classrooms.

  • A short walk from home, on a steep path.
  • Parents say: “The teachers here know every child by name.”
  • But there are few children, and the hamlet is hard to reach.
2 The decision

The district must decide whether to merge Kasta

If Kasta closes, 22 children need another school. The rules say a school for classes I to V should be within 1 km walking. The state is merging small schools into schools 2 to 5 km away. So the question is not “which is nearest?” but “which is safe, supported and possible?”

  • The Dobhi khad (stream) fills within an hour of heavy rain. A “short” walk becomes a danger on the days it matters most.
  • Two problems were confirmed in the field check (sample answers): the crossing is not passable in heavy rain, and no public transport serves school hours.
  • Making it work needs an escort for the youngest children and a monsoon learning point: about ₹1,32,000 a year.

6 Allowed by the rules

A farther school can be made possible by the right policies

RTE Rule 6(1)(a)

“…for classes I to V, a school… within a walking distance of one km of the neighbourhood.”

Soyal is beyond 1 km, so the journey needs planned support, not a hope.

Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Rules, 2010

RTE Rule 6(3)

“In places with difficult terrain, risk of landslides, floods… the appropriate Government… shall locate the school in such a manner as to avoid such dangers.”

This is why the flooding stream at Dobhi counts, even though Dobhi is nearer.

RTE Rules, 2010

Samagra Shiksha transport and escort

“…up to an average cost @ Rs. 6000 per child per annum… appraised… as per the distance, the terrain and the type of transport facility.”

22 children × ₹6,000 = ₹1,32,000 a year at most, if no bus already runs.

Samagra Shiksha, programmatic and financial norms

Himachal's own merger plan merges small schools within 2 to 5 km, and extends the distance to 3 km where no other school exists in range (The News Himachal, June 2025). A High Court upheld one merger because the new school was easy to reach (LiveLaw, September 2026): access is what courts look at, so access is what PathShala documents.

7 After

Asha's new journey

Before
  • A short, steep walk to GPS Kasta
  • 22 children, 2 teachers
  • A steep, hard-to-reach hillside hamlet
  • Parents worried about a school with too few children
After
  • A pickup point where the road begins, so the youngest do not walk the steep path
  • A ride on a metalled road to GPS Soyal
  • A bigger school with room for all 22 children
  • A monsoon and winter plan agreed in advance
How PathShala reached this. It reads citizen messages (Hindi and English), the terrain around each school, the route and bus timetable, the policy text and the cost calculator, and shows the reasons for each step. It only suggests: nothing is decided or submitted until the officer acts.
